Most organisations reach the same point. Coaching works for the few leaders who get it, so the request comes: can we offer it more widely? The budget usually says no. Certifying some of your own people as internal coaches is one answer. Buying more external coaching is another. Each solves a different problem.

## When external coaching is the better choice

External coaches bring independence. A senior executive working through a difficult board relationship, a restructure or their own career may not want to talk to a colleague, however well trained. External coaching also suits situations where the topic is politically sensitive, where you need a specialist quickly, or where only one or two people need support.

The trade-off is cost and reach. You pay for every session, every year, and the capability leaves when the engagement ends.

## When internal coaches are the better choice

Internal coaches know your culture, systems and pressures. They can support managers and teams across the organisation, not just the top tier, and they're available when a problem first appears rather than after a procurement process. The skills also show up in their everyday work: a manager trained to coach has better development and performance conversations even when nobody calls it coaching.

The trade-off is upfront investment and the need for clear boundaries. Internal coaches need proper training, confidentiality agreements that people trust, and a clear line between coaching and the support your EAP provides.

> External coaching buys sessions. Certifying internal coaches builds a capability that stays.

## Five questions to help you decide

1. **Who needs coaching?** A handful of executives points to external coaches. Managers and teams across the business point to internal capability.
2. **How sensitive are the topics?** The more confidential or political the issues, the stronger the case for an outsider.
3. **How long is the need?** A one-off transition suits external support. An ongoing need, such as developing every new manager, suits internal coaches.
4. **Do you have the right people?** Internal coaches work best when they have credibility, empathy and time. Choose them carefully, and get their managers' agreement before they start.
5. **Can you protect confidentiality?** If staff won't trust an internal coach, the program won't work. Set clear agreements before the first session.

## Most organisations use both

In practice the answer is rarely one or the other. A common model keeps external coaches for the executive team and builds a group of certified internal coaches for managers and teams. Coaching reaches more people, and the external budget goes where independence matters most.

To make the case internally, compare the yearly cost of external coaching for the people you want to reach with the cost of certifying your own coaches, and decide in advance how you'll measure the difference.
