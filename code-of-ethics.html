Code of Ethics

The standards every ITL faculty member, student and graduate agrees to uphold.

Version 1.0 · Last updated [DATE]

Who this code applies to

This code applies to ITL faculty and mentor coaches, to students while they study, and to graduates who hold an ITL credential. Agreeing to it is a condition of enrolment and of keeping your credential.

Our five principles

Integrity. Be honest about what coaching can and cannot do, and about your own skills and credentials.

Do no harm. Put the wellbeing of the person first. Stop, or refer on, when coaching is not what they need.

Loyalty to the person. Your duty of care is to the person you coach, even when an organisation pays.

Respect and inclusion. Treat every person with dignity, and work in culturally safe ways.

Competence. Practise only within your training, and keep your skills current.

1. Scope of practice

Coaching supports people with goals, performance and wellbeing at work. ITL coaches do not diagnose or treat mental health conditions, and do not present coaching as therapy or counselling.

If a person shows signs of a mental health condition, distress beyond the scope of coaching, or risk of harm, you will pause coaching and help them connect with appropriate support, such as their EAP, GP or a crisis service.

2. Confidentiality and its limits

Keep coaching conversations confidential. Explain the limits of confidentiality at the start of every coaching relationship. You may share information without consent only when:

  • there is a serious risk of harm to the person or to others
  • the law requires it
  • the person has agreed in writing, for example in a three-way agreement with their organisation

3. Clear agreements

Agree the purpose, length, confidentiality and any reporting before coaching begins. When an organisation sponsors coaching, make a clear three-way agreement with the person and their sponsor about what will and will not be shared.

4. Conflicts of interest

Avoid coaching relationships that compromise objectivity or trust. Internal coaches in particular should not coach:

  • their own direct reports
  • people whose performance, pay or employment they decide or influence
  • close friends or family members

Disclose any conflict as soon as you become aware of it, and refer the person to another coach if needed.

5. Honest representation

Describe your ITL credential accurately: a professional certification, not a nationally recognised qualification or a clinical qualification. Never guarantee specific results from coaching.

6. Records and recordings

Record a session only with written consent. Store notes and recordings securely, keep only what you need, and delete them when they are no longer required.

7. Ongoing development and supervision

Keep developing your skills, and seek supervision or peer support, especially when coaching raises difficult or emotional issues.

8. Safety

If someone is in immediate danger, call 000. For crisis support, people can call Lifeline on 13 11 14.

9. Raising concerns and breaches

Anyone can raise a concern about the conduct of an ITL faculty member, student or graduate through our complaints process. ITL will investigate fairly and give the person concerned a chance to respond.

Depending on the outcome, ITL may require further training or supervision, or suspend or withdraw an ITL credential.
