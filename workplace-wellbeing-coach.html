Why this matters now

Organisations need wellbeing that works, not wellness days.

Australian employers now have legal duties to manage psychosocial hazards like high job demands. Yet most wellbeing programs are one-off sessions with no evidence base and no way to show they made a difference.

This certification prepares HR, People and Culture and wellbeing professionals, people leaders and practitioners with corporate backgrounds to deliver programs grounded in evidence, and to measure what changed.

Outcomes

When you graduate, you'll be able to

Lead mindfulness sessions

Guide groups and individuals through practical, secular mindfulness practices suited to a workday.

Apply ACT-informed techniques

Help people step back from stressful thoughts, clarify their values and act on them.

Explain stress and recovery

Translate the neuroscience of stress, attention and recovery into plain language for any audience.

Design a wellbeing program

Build programs that fit a corporate calendar and support psychosocial risk controls.

Measure wellbeing outcomes

Use validated wellbeing measures before and after, and report results in business terms.

Respond safely to distress

Recognise warning signs, respond to disclosures and refer to EAP or clinical care.

Curriculum

Six modules, from the science to the session.

Each module pairs online learning with live facilitation practice and a workplace application task.

  1. 01

    Stress, burnout and the neuroscience of recovery

    How the stress response works, what chronic load does to the brain and body, and what genuine recovery requires.

  2. 02

    Mindfulness practice and facilitation

    Core practices, how to guide them well, and adapting them for busy, sceptical or digitally overloaded audiences.

  3. 03

    ACT in the workplace

    Psychological flexibility, defusion, values and committed action, applied to workplace stress and change.

  4. 04

    Attention, digital load and focus

    The research on task switching and constant connectivity, and practical strategies for focus and boundaries.

  5. 05

    Designing workplace wellbeing programs

    Needs analysis, program design, psychosocial risk context, and getting buy-in from leaders and HR.

  6. 06

    Safety, scope and measurement

    Trauma-sensitive practice, responding to disclosures, referral pathways, privacy, and measuring outcomes.

The evidence base

Built on research, not wellness trends.

HR and WHS teams will ask what your program is based on. You'll have a clear answer.

Mindfulness-based programs

Structured, secular programs studied for stress reduction and attention.

Acceptance and Commitment Therapy

Research on psychological flexibility, including workplace-adapted training.

Neuroscience of stress

How chronic stress affects attention, memory and decision-making.

Psychosocial risk frameworks

Safe Work Australia's model Code of Practice for managing psychosocial hazards.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is this therapy or counselling training?

No. This is a non-clinical certification for workplace wellbeing programs. You'll use techniques informed by mindfulness and ACT to support healthy people at work, not to diagnose or treat mental health conditions.

Do I need a psychology or counselling background?

No. You need meaningful corporate experience, empathy and a commitment to ethical practice. The program teaches the science and the methods, and where your scope ends.

Is mindfulness actually evidence-based, or just a trend?

Structured mindfulness programs and ACT have been widely studied, including in workplaces. Results vary with program quality and context, which is why we teach you to design carefully and measure outcomes rather than assume them.

Do I need my own meditation practice?

You don't need years of experience to enrol. You'll build a personal practice during the program, because credible facilitators practise what they teach.

What if someone in a session discloses a mental health crisis?

You'll learn exactly what to do: how to respond calmly, keep the person safe, and connect them with the organisation's EAP, their GP or crisis services. Safe referral is assessed before you graduate.

Will this help my organisation meet psychosocial obligations?

Wellbeing programs can support an organisation's psychosocial risk controls, but they don't replace fixing the causes of hazards such as workload or role clarity. You'll learn where programs fit within a broader risk approach. This isn't legal advice.

Can I deliver programs online?

Yes. You'll learn to facilitate both in person and online, including keeping remote participants engaged.

How much time will it take?

Plan for a few hours a week, plus practicum. Live sessions run outside core business hours and are recorded.

Can my employer pay?

Yes. We invoice organisations directly and can provide a summary of learning outcomes to support your request.

Will I get paid work afterwards?

We can't promise work. We do teach you to design programs and report outcomes, which is what organisations look for when they hire. Graduates can also apply to join ITL's practitioner network as it grows.

What's your refund policy?

Cancellations, refunds and transfers are covered in our enrolment terms. Contact us for more information.
