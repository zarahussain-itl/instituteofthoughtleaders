ITL Coaching Competency Standard

What competent coaching looks like at ITL, and exactly what assessors look for in your observed sessions. It is built on the eight ICF Core Competencies, with three extra competencies for coaching inside organisations.

The ICF Core Competencies are developed and owned by the International Coaching Federation. ITL's standard is aligned with them; it is not issued or endorsed by the ICF.

How you're rated

Assessors rate each competency on a four-level scale, using recordings or live observation of your coaching sessions.

LevelWhat it means
1 · Not yet evidentThe competency is missing or works against the session.
2 · DevelopingPresent at times, but inconsistent or led by the coach's agenda.
3 · CompetentClearly and consistently shown, in a way that serves the person being coached. This is the pass level.
4 · StrongShown with skill and flexibility, adapting naturally to the person and the moment.

To pass

  • Reach Competent or above on every competency, across your assessed sessions.
  • Competencies 1 (ethical practice) and W1 (scope and referral) must reach Competent in every session. A safety concern in any session means "not yet competent", whatever the other ratings.
  • Every result comes with written feedback. If you don't pass, you can resit once at no extra cost.

Domain A

Foundation

1. Demonstrates ethical practice

Acts with integrity and follows the ITL Code of Ethics, especially on confidentiality and honesty.

Assessors look for

  • Confidentiality and its limits explained in plain language
  • No advice or claims beyond the coach's competence
  • Respect for the person's own choices and values

2. Embodies a coaching mindset

Stays curious, open and focused on the person, and treats them as capable of finding their own way forward.

Assessors look for

  • Questions rather than solutions offered by default
  • Awareness of the coach's own reactions and biases
  • Reflection on the session afterwards, in the coach's log

Domain B

Co-creating the relationship

3. Establishes and maintains agreements

Agrees what the session and the relationship are for, and checks progress against it.

Assessors look for

  • A clear, agreed focus early in the session
  • A shared sense of what success looks like today
  • Checking in on the agreement as the session moves

4. Cultivates trust and safety

Creates a relationship where the person feels safe to speak openly.

Assessors look for

  • Genuine acknowledgement of the person's strengths and effort
  • Support without judgement when difficult feelings come up
  • Respect for the person's identity, culture and context

5. Maintains presence

Stays fully focused, calm and responsive to what is happening in the moment.

Assessors look for

  • Following the person's lead rather than a script
  • Comfort with silence and uncertainty
  • Staying steady when emotions run high

Domain C

Communicating effectively

6. Listens actively

Hears what is said, what is meant and what is left out.

Assessors look for

  • Summaries and reflections in the person's own words
  • Noticing tone, energy and body language
  • Exploring the meaning behind key words or patterns

7. Evokes awareness

Helps the person see their situation in new ways, using questions, reflection and evidence-based tools such as CBT-informed reframing.

Assessors look for

  • Open, concise questions that move thinking forward
  • Challenging assumptions respectfully
  • Helping the person name their own insights

Domain D

Cultivating learning and growth

8. Facilitates client growth

Turns insight into action the person owns, and supports them to follow through.

Assessors look for

  • Actions designed by the person, not handed to them
  • Thought given to obstacles and support
  • A clear close that links back to the agreed focus

ITL workplace practice

Three competencies for coaching inside organisations

ITL adds these because workplace coaches answer to more than one party, meet people under pressure, and need to show results.

W1. Works within scope and refers safely

  • Recognises signs of distress or risk
  • Pauses coaching and refers to EAP, GP or crisis support
  • Never diagnoses or offers treatment

W2. Contracts with organisations

  • Sets clear three-way agreements with sponsors
  • Agrees what is reported and what stays confidential
  • Manages conflicts of interest as an internal coach

W3. Measures and reports outcomes

  • Agrees measures with the person and sponsor at the start
  • Tracks progress during the engagement
  • Reports results honestly, without overclaiming

How the standard applies to each certification

Corporate Performance Coach: all eleven competencies, assessed in one-to-one coaching sessions.

Executive and Team Coach: all eleven, assessed at a higher expected level, in executive sessions and an observed team coaching session.

Workplace Wellbeing Coach: all eleven, plus group facilitation, assessed in an observed wellbeing session. W1 carries extra weight.
