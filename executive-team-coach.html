Is this you?

You've led people. Now coach the people who lead.

Organisations promote strong managers into executive roles every year, and many stall or leave because nobody helped them make the shift. Teams lose months to unclear decisions, digital overload and meetings that go nowhere. Executive and team coaching addresses both, and it's where the most valuable coaching contracts sit.

This certification is for experienced leaders, HR and L&D professionals and existing coaches who want to work at that level, with a method aligned to the ICF Core Competencies.

Outcomes

When you graduate, you'll be able to

Coach executive transitions

Help new executives build the mindset, presence and strategic focus their role demands.

Coach whole teams

Work with a team as a system, inside its real meetings, on how it communicates and decides.

Manage three-way contracts

Align the coachee, their manager and the sponsor on goals, confidentiality and measures.

Coach to the ICF Core Competencies

Demonstrate ethical practice, coaching presence, active listening and evoking awareness.

Build psychological safety

Help teams raise problems early, disagree well and learn from mistakes.

Prove coaching ROI

Report outcomes in the business terms executives and boards expect.

Frequently asked questions

Will this give me an ICF credential?

The program is aligned with the ICF Core Competencies, so you'll learn and be assessed to the same standard. ICF credentials such as ACC and PCC are awarded by the ICF and require training from an ICF-accredited program, mentor coaching and logged coaching hours. ITL's ICF accreditation status: [STATUS]. Until accreditation is confirmed, these hours may not count towards an ICF credential.

Do I need coaching experience first?

You need either leadership experience, prior coach training, or ITL's Corporate Performance Coach. If you're new to coaching altogether, we recommend starting with Corporate Performance Coach.

I've never been an executive. Can I coach one?

Yes. Executive coaches aren't advisers who've done the job before. They help leaders think more clearly about their own challenges. Your corporate experience gives you credibility, and the program teaches you how to hold your ground with senior, demanding clients.

How is team coaching different from facilitation?

A facilitator runs a session. A team coach works with a team over time on how it operates: how it decides, communicates and handles conflict, often coaching live in its real meetings. The goal is a team that keeps improving after the coach leaves.
